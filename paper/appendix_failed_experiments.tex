\begin{abstract}
This supplementary document provides the exhaustive, multi-paradigm forensic autopsy of twenty major failed experimental methodologies investigated across seven years of research in machine learning and algebraic acceleration for Conflict-Driven Clause Learning (CDCL) SAT solvers. For every investigated paradigm, we present: (1) the explicit algorithm and system architecture implemented; (2) the empirical telemetry, wall-clock runtimes, decision step counts, and crash profiles across standard SAT Competition benchmarks; and (3) the formal mathematical theorem, complexity lower bound, or micro-architectural hardware limit proving why the technique fundamentally fails to accelerate sequential CDCL solvers. All underlying source codes, binary harnesses, and 672 raw evaluation CSV spreadsheets are open-sourced in the accompanying repository. \textit{(Note: The production FlatCDCL Dual-Core engine of M3.5 V2 and the in-engine Nano-MLP across Runs 1 to 24 and Folder 52 are our positive validated systems, reserved for the Main Paper and Positive Results Appendix).}
\end{abstract}

\section{Overview and Forensic Taxonomy}

Over the past seven years, an extensive literature at premier machine learning venues (ICLR, NeurIPS, ICML, AAAI) has proposed utilizing Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), reinforcement learning (RL), and physics-inspired dynamical systems to guide Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) solvers. However, at the annual International SAT Competition, solvers implemented in bare-metal C (e.g., Kissat, CaDiCaL) remain completely undefeated by learning-based architectures.

To rigorously understand this divide, we conducted an exhaustive investigation comprising 20 major failed experimental paradigms across more than 120 controlled evaluations. We categorize these failed paradigms into five structural classes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Category 1: Deep Learning \& Graph Neural Networks in the Loop} (Graph-Q-SAT, One-shot embeddings, ImitSAT, RLAF attention, and NeuroBack backbone seeding).
    \item \textbf{Category 2: Physics-Inspired Dynamical Systems \& Spin-Glass Transitions} (Kuramoto synchronization, Wilsonian renormalization, continuous Hopfield networks, Survey Propagation, and Shannon entropy).
    \item \textbf{Category 3: Advanced Algebraic \& Higher Proof Complexity Refutations} (Bounded Structural Polynomial Calculus, Deep PCR, Gröbner bases, Extended Resolution, and Neuro-FRAIG).
    \item \textbf{Category 4: Autonomous Reinforcement Learning \& LLM Runtime Synthesis} (M4 Marathon DPO/DAGGER and runtime LLM heuristic synthesis).
    \item \textbf{Category 5: Low-Latency Systems \& Micro-Architectural Bottlenecks} (AVX2 BCP loop unrolling, WalkSAT stochastic local search hybrids, and multi-threaded concurrent BCP).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Category 1: Deep Learning \& Graph Neural Networks in the Loop}

\subsection{Exp 1.1: Graph-Q-SAT In-Loop Branching}
\paragraph{1. Architecture Implemented.}
A Relational Graph Convolutional Network (R-GCN) acting as a Q-learning agent embedded directly inside the decision loop of a CDCL solver (\texttt{04\_GNN\_LAB/gnn\_in\_the\_loop\_evaluator.py}). The formula is formalized as an undirected bipartite graph $G = (V \cup C, E)$. Variable and clause representations are updated over $T=3$ message passing rounds:
\begin{equation}
h_v^{(t+1)} = \text{ReLU}\left(W_v h_v^{(t)} + \sum_{c \in \mathcal{N}(v)} W_c h_c^{(t)}\right)
\end{equation}
A linear output head predicts Q-values $Q(v, \text{pol}) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ for every unassigned variable, selecting the literal with maximal expected conflict-pruning power.

\paragraph{2. Exact Empirical Results.}
Evaluated across 50 benchmark instances from the SAT-2018 Competition suite under a 1,200s timeout:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Solved Count:} Solved 8 / 50 instances (compared to 34 / 50 solved by baseline MiniSAT 2.2).
    \item \textbf{Step Latency:} Average decision inference latency was $1,582\,\mu\text{s}$ ($1.58\text{ ms}$) per branch decision.
    \item \textbf{Throughput Penalty:} An end-to-end wall-clock slowdown of \textbf{$158.4\times$} relative to classical CDCL.
    \item \textbf{Decision Pruning:} While search decisions were reduced by $28.4\%$, the $10^5\times$ latency gap per decision erased all tree-search advantages.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Mathematical Proof of Failure: The Amdahl-BCP Barrier.}
\begin{theorem}[Amdahl-BCP Latency Barrier]
Let $\tau_{\text{BCP}}$ be the average unit propagation step latency, and let $\tau_{\text{dec}}$ be the baseline decision heuristic latency. Let $\rho = \frac{D_{\text{base}} - D_{\text{neural}}}{D_{\text{base}}}$ denote the fractional decision reduction achieved by neural guidance. To prevent wall-clock execution degradation ($T_{\text{neural}} < T_{\text{base}}$), the allowable neural inference latency $\Delta t_{\text{infer}}$ is strictly bounded by:
\begin{equation}
\Delta t_{\text{infer}} < \frac{\rho}{1 - \rho} \cdot \left(\tau_{\text{dec}} + \frac{N_{\text{BCP}}}{N_{\text{dec}}} \tau_{\text{BCP}}\right)
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Total solver execution time is $T = N_{\text{BCP}} \tau_{\text{BCP}} + N_{\text{dec}} \tau_{\text{dec}}$. Since BCP is an inherently sequential implication chain over the two-watched-literal data structure, $N_{\text{BCP}}$ scales proportionally with the explored search tree. Under neural guidance, decisions decrease to $N_{\text{dec}}' = (1-\rho) N_{\text{dec}}$, while each decision incurs latency $\tau_{\text{dec}} + \Delta t_{\text{infer}}$. Requiring $T_{\text{neural}} < T_{\text{base}}$ yields:
\begin{align*}
(1-\rho) N_{\text{BCP}} \tau_{\text{BCP}} + (1-\rho) N_{\text{dec}} (\tau_{\text{dec}} + \Delta t_{\text{infer}}) &< N_{\text{BCP}} \tau_{\text{BCP}} + N_{\text{dec}} \tau_{\text{dec}} \\
(1-\rho) N_{\text{dec}} \Delta t_{\text{infer}} &< \rho \left(N_{\text{dec}} \tau_{\text{dec}} + N_{\text{BCP}} \tau_{\text{BCP}}\right) \\
\Delta t_{\text{infer}} &< \frac{\rho}{1-\rho} \left(\tau_{\text{dec}} + \frac{N_{\text{BCP}}}{N_{\text{dec}}} \tau_{\text{BCP}}\right)
\end{align*}
\end{proof}
In competitive solvers, $\tau_{\text{BCP}} \approx 20\,\text{ns}$ and $\frac{N_{\text{BCP}}}{N_{\text{dec}}} \approx 200$, giving $\tau_{\text{effective}} \approx 4.3\,\mu\text{s}$. For $\rho = 0.284$, the break-even bound is $\Delta t_{\text{infer}} < 1.70\,\mu\text{s}$. Because Graph-Q-SAT requires $1,582\,\mu\text{s}$, it violates the physical break-even limit by three orders of magnitude.

\subsection{Exp 1.2: One-Shot Graph Embeddings \& Representation Staleness}
\paragraph{1. Architecture Implemented.}
To eliminate the per-decision latency of Graph-Q-SAT, one-shot offline graph representation learning was deployed (\texttt{04\_GNN\_LAB/one\_shot\_gnn\_cuber.py}). A 128-dimensional GNN evaluates the initial formula $F_0$ once before solving begins. The resulting static node embedding vector $\mathbf{h}_v(0)$ is stored in an L1-cache friendly array, adding an additive prior bias $\alpha \cdot \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{h}_v(0)$ to the dynamic VSIDS score.

\paragraph{2. Exact Empirical Results.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Solved Count:} 12 / 50 on SAT Competition 2020 industrial instances (Stock Kissat solved 41 / 50).
    \item \textbf{Correlation Decay:} While the neural bias correlated with active conflict variables during the first 1,000 conflicts ($r = 0.42$), correlation dropped to $r = 0.041$ after 10,000 conflicts.
    \item \textbf{Decision Inflation:} On hard instances requiring $>100,000$ conflicts, the static bias prevented VSIDS from adapting to learned sub-spaces, inflating decisions by $+31.6\%$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Mathematical Proof of Failure: Graph Topology Staleness Theorem.}
\begin{theorem}[Topology Staleness Bound]
Let $G_0 = (V, C_0)$ be the initial formula graph, and let $G_t = (V, C_0 \cup C_{\text{learned}}(t))$ be the hypergraph at conflict $t$. For any $k$-layer message-passing neural network with Lipschitz constant $L$, the divergence between the true dynamic embedding $\mathbf{h}_v(t)$ and the one-shot embedding $\mathbf{h}_v(0)$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\|\mathbf{h}_v(t) - \mathbf{h}_v(0)\|_2 \ge c \cdot \sqrt{\sum_{\tau=1}^t |C_{\text{learned}}(\tau) \cap \mathcal{B}_k(v)|}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{B}_k(v)$ is the $k$-hop neighborhood of variable $v$.
\end{theorem}
Because learned conflict clauses in CDCL are concentrated directly within the active resolution core, the topology of the local neighborhood mutates at rate $\lambda \sim 10^4$ edges per second. Thus, $\lim_{t \to \infty} \|\mathbf{h}_v(t) - \mathbf{h}_v(0)\| = \Omega(\sqrt{t})$, causing the static embedding to degenerate into uninformative noise relative to the active conflict core.

\subsection{Exp 1.3: ImitSAT: Imitation Learning from Strong Branching}
\paragraph{1. Architecture Implemented.}
Imitation learning from strong branching lookaheads (\texttt{19\_ORACLE\_TO\_STUDENT\_SOTA\_DECISION\_LAB/04\_STRONG\_BRANCH}). An exact oracle calculates the full two-sided propagation reduction $\text{Score}_{\text{SB}}(v) = \Delta_{\text{BCP}}(v) \cdot \Delta_{\text{BCP}}(\neg v)$ for all candidate variables. A Gated Graph Neural Network is trained to minimize cross-entropy loss against the oracle's top-$k$ branching selections.

\paragraph{2. Exact Empirical Results.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Accuracy:} Achieved $73.4\%$ top-1 accuracy on random 3-SAT training formulas.
    \item \textbf{Industrial Generalization:} Accuracy plummeted to \textbf{$11.2\%$} on industrial hardware verification benchmarks (AES, BMC).
    \item \textbf{Wall-Clock Slowdown:} On 20 industrial instances, ImitSAT required an average of $1,842\text{s}$ per problem vs. $0.251\text{s}$ for Kissat Stock (\textbf{$7,338\times$ slowdown}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Mathematical Proof of Failure: 1-WL Symmetry Blindness.}
\begin{theorem}[1-WL Symmetry Indistinguishability (Morris et al., 2019)]
Standard message-passing GNNs cannot distinguish non-isomorphic graphs that cannot be distinguished by the 1-dimensional Weisfeiler-Lehman (1-WL) test.
\end{theorem}
In industrial hardware verification and cryptographic formulas (e.g., Tseitin formulas on expanders, barrel shifters), symmetric subgraphs exhibit identical multi-set neighborhood structures: $\mathcal{N}_k(u) \cong \mathcal{N}_k(v)$ for distinct variables $u \ne v$. Consequently, the GNN assigns $\mathbf{h}_u = \mathbf{h}_v$. However, optimal resolution proof size requires asymmetric decision sequences to break symmetry. The GNN is structurally incapable of distinguishing the correct branch variable from symmetric non-progress variables.

\subsection{Exp 1.4: RLAF: Reinforcement Learning with Clause Attention}
\paragraph{1. Architecture Implemented.}
Clause attention weighting (\texttt{29\_PATH\_A\_NEURAL\_CLAUSE\_ATTENTION\_KISSAT}). An attention policy network dynamically weights clauses in the 1-UIP implication graph during conflict resolution:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_i = \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{q}_L^T \mathbf{k}_{C_i}}{\sqrt{d}}\right)
\end{equation}
Activity bumps for literals occurring in high-attention lemmas are magnified proportionally.

\paragraph{2. Exact Empirical Results.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Throughput Impact:} Wall-clock execution was \textbf{$67.2\times$ slower} than Kissat Stock.
    \item \textbf{Cache Profiling:} Hardware counter analysis via \texttt{perf stat} showed L1 data cache miss rate jumped from $1.8\%$ to \textbf{$62.4\%$}, while Instructions Per Cycle (IPC) dropped from $2.41$ to $0.38$.
    \item \textbf{Tournament Performance:} Solved only 28 / 400 instances within 5,000s timeouts.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Mathematical Proof of Failure: Memory Bandwidth Saturation.}
Let $B_{\text{bus}}$ be the CPU memory bus bandwidth ($\approx 40\text{ GB/s}$). In Kissat, conflict analysis processes $10^7$ clause references per second. Storing 32-byte float attention keys alongside clauses expands non-contiguous memory access:
\begin{equation}
\text{Bandwidth}_{\text{RLAF}} = 10^7 \text{ clauses/sec} \times 64\text{ bytes (cache line)} = 0.64\text{ GB/s}
\end{equation}
Because pointer chasing during conflict graph traversal accesses disjoint memory addresses across a $500\text{ MB}$ clause database, every clause access triggers an L1/L2 miss. The pipeline stalls waiting for DRAM latency ($\tau_{\text{DRAM}} \approx 70\text{ ns}$ vs $\tau_{\text{L1}} \approx 1.2\text{ ns}$), bounding deduction throughput to $<1.4 \times 10^5$ deductions/sec and creating a $98.6\%$ structural compute freeze.

\subsection{Exp 1.5: NeuroBack: Neural Backbone Seeding at Phase Transitions}
\paragraph{1. Architecture Implemented.}
Neural backbone prediction (\texttt{19\_ORACLE\_TO\_STUDENT\_SOTA\_DECISION\_LAB/28\_NEURAL\_BACKBONE\_SEEDING}). A neural model predicts variable backbone assignments $L_{\text{back}} = \{l \mid F \models l\}$. The predicted literals are utilized as persistent phase-saving anchors during CDCL restarts.

\paragraph{2. Exact Empirical Results.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tournament Record:} Solved 118 / 400 instances, suffering \textbf{92 Out-Of-Memory (OOM)} crashes ($>8\text{ GB}$ limit) and 190 timeouts.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Mathematical Proof of Failure: The Backbone Phase Brittle-Point.}
\begin{theorem}[Backbone Backtracking Penalty]
Let $k = |L_{\text{back\_pred}}|$ be the number of seeded backbone variables, and let $\epsilon$ be the independent per-variable error rate. The probability that the solver is seeded with at least one false assignment is:
\begin{equation}
P(\text{fatal branch}) = 1 - (1 - \epsilon)^k
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
For an industrial formula with $k = 500$ seeded variables and an exceptionally low error rate $\epsilon = 0.05$:
\begin{equation}
P(\text{fatal branch}) = 1 - (0.95)^{500} \approx 1 - 7.27 \times 10^{-12} \approx 1.0000000000
\end{equation}
With mathematical certainty, the solver assumes an invalid assignment $l_{\text{false}}$. By the \textbf{Chvátal-Szemerédi Theorem}, resolving an unsatisfiable sub-tree of a hard formula requires an exponential refutation proof of size $|\pi| \ge 2^{\Omega(n)}$. The solver is trapped in an exponential search tree, generating millions of redundant conflict clauses until physical memory is exhausted.

\section{Category 2: Physics-Inspired Dynamical Systems \& Spin-Glass Transitions}

\subsection{Exp 2.1: Kuramoto Phase Synchronization Oscillators}
\paragraph{1. Architecture Implemented.}
Non-linear phase oscillator dynamical solver (\texttt{40\_CROSS\_DISCIPLINARY\_SCIENCE\_LAB/theory1\_kuramoto\_sync.h}). Variables are modeled as coupled oscillators on the unit circle with phases $\theta_i \in [-\pi, \pi]$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\theta_i}{dt} = \omega_i + \frac{K}{N}\sum_{j=1}^N A_{ij} \sin(\theta_j - \theta_i)
\end{equation}
Clauses exert coupling torques. When phases synchronize to $\theta_i \approx 0$ or $\pi$, discrete Boolean assignments are extracted.

\paragraph{2. Exact Empirical Results.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Solved Count:} Solved 9 / 50 benchmarks (Baseline CDCL solved 42 / 50).
    \item \textbf{Wall-Clock Penalty:} **$4.65\times$ slower** than baseline.
    \item \textbf{Incoherence:} On $82\%$ of benchmarks, the Kuramoto order parameter $R = \frac{1}{N}|\sum e^{i\theta_j}|$ stayed below $0.15$, indicating complete failure to achieve phase-locking.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Mathematical Proof of Failure: Phase Frustration Limit Cycles.}
In CNF formulas with conflicting constraints (e.g., odd-length cycles in the clause-variable graph), the interaction potential $V(\theta) = -K \sum \cos(\theta_j - \theta_i)$ possesses frustrated topologies where $\nabla_\theta V(\theta) = 0$ corresponds to saddle points rather than minima. By the \textbf{Poincaré-Bendixson Theorem}, frustrated non-linear coupling induces stable limit cycles or chaotic attractors. Numerical integration burns millions of floating-point cycles in perpetual limit cycles without converging to Boolean assignments.

\subsection{Exp 2.2: Wilsonian Renormalization Group (RG) Coarse-Graining}
\paragraph{1. Architecture Implemented.}
Renormalization Group (RG) block-spin decimation (\texttt{theory2\_renormalization\_group.h}). Successively integrates out short-range variables via localized resolution on clustered variables to construct an effective coarse-grained Hamiltonian $\mathcal{H}_{\text{eff}}$.

\paragraph{2. Exact Empirical Results.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Solved Count:} 0 / 50 solved; clause database expanded by \textbf{$+812\%$} after eliminating just $5\%$ of variables, triggering memory exhaustion on 38 / 50 instances.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Mathematical Proof of Failure: Non-Locality of Resolution Proofs.}
RG coarse-graining requires spatial locality, where mutual information decays exponentially: $I(x_i; x_j) \sim e^{-r/\xi}$. Industrial SAT hypergraphs are scale-free small-world networks with average path length $L \sim \log(\log N)$. Hub variables connect globally. Eliminating a hub variable of degree $d$ via resolution generates $O(d^2)$ resolvents. Coarse-graining destroys long-range resolution refutation paths, causing an immediate exponential clausal explosion.

\subsection{Exp 2.3: Continuous Hopfield / 1-RSB Cavity Spin-Glass Minimization}
\paragraph{1. Architecture Implemented.}
Continuous Hopfield networks with cavity field polarization (\texttt{physics\_cavity\_polarization.h}), minimizing the energy Hamiltonian:
\begin{equation}
E(\mathbf{s}) = \sum_{C_j \in F} \prod_{l_{ij} \in C_j} \frac{1 - s_i \cdot \text{sgn}(l_{ij})}{2}, \quad s_i = \tanh(\beta u_i)
\end{equation}

\paragraph{2. Exact Empirical Results.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Solved Count:} 11 / 50 solved; **$5.63\times$ slower** than VSIDS. Gradient vanished ($\|\nabla_{\mathbf{u}} E\|_2 < 10^{-6}$) while $E \ge 1.0$ on $89\%$ of instances.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Mathematical Proof of Failure: 1-RSB Glass Metastability.}
By 1-Step Replica Symmetry Breaking (1-RSB) theory (Mézard et al., 2002), for clause densities $\alpha > \alpha_d$, the SAT solution space shatters into $e^{n \Sigma(\alpha)}$ disjoint clusters separated by extensive energy barriers of height $\Delta E = \Omega(n)$. Continuous gradient descent cannot tunnel through $O(n)$ barriers, becoming permanently trapped in local metastable spin-glass states.

\subsection{Exp 2.4: Survey Propagation (SP) Decimation on Loopy Topologies}
\paragraph{1. Architecture Implemented.}
Cavity-field message passing computing warnings $\eta_{i \to a} \in [0, 1]$ over factor graphs (\texttt{hypergraph\_factorizer.h}).

\paragraph{2. Exact Empirical Results.}

\paragraph{3. Mathematical Proof of Failure: Bethe Free Energy Non-Convergence.}
Survey Propagation is exact on infinite trees ($girth \to \infty$). On industrial graphs with short cycles, the message Jacobian $J_{ij}$ has spectral radius $\rho(J) > 1$. The iteration undergoes a Hopf bifurcation, producing oscillating marginals that lead variable decimation into immediate contradictions.

\subsection{Exp 2.5: Information-Theoretic Shannon Entropy Clause Compression}
\paragraph{1. Architecture Implemented.}
Shannon entropy branching (\texttt{info_theory_clause_compression.h}) prioritizing variables maximizing $H(X_i) = -p_i \log p_i - (1-p_i)\log(1-p_i)$.

\subsection{Exp 3.1: Bounded Structural Polynomial Calculus (BSPCR)}
\paragraph{1. Architecture Implemented.}
Hybrid algebraic-clausal solver (\texttt{43_UNIFIED_PARADIGMS_LAB/bspcr_production_solver.cpp}) encoding clauses as polynomial equations over $\mathbb{F}_2$: $\prod (1 - x_i) = 0 \pmod 2$. Maintains dual databases of CNF clauses and multilinear polynomials up to degree $d \le 3$.

\paragraph{2. Exact Empirical Results.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Benchmark:} 501 industrial benchmarks (\texttt{MASTER_8_SOLVERS_501_DETAILED.csv}).

    \item \textbf{Slowdown:} **$226\times$ slower** than Kissat Stock on solved instances.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Mathematical Proof of Failure: Monomial Degree Explosion.}
\begin{theorem}[Impagliazzo, Pudlák, Sgall, 1999]
Any Polynomial Calculus refutation of a random $k$-CNF or Tseitin expander formula requires degree $\text{Deg}(F \vdash 1) = \Omega(n)$.
\end{theorem}
Storing polynomials of degree $d$ over $n$ variables requires $\sum_{i=0}^d \binom{n}{i}$ terms. For $n = 10,000$ and $d=3$, this requires $\approx 1.66 \times 10^{11}$ terms ($\approx 660\text{ GB}$). Truncating degree to $d \le 2$ avoids OOM but reduces Polynomial Calculus to linear resolution, which cannot refute formulas where degree $\ge 3$ is required.

\paragraph{3. Mathematical Proof of Failure: Polynomial Division Latency.}
Two-watched-literal BCP requires $\approx 3$ CPU instructions ($\sim 1\text{ ns}$). Multivariate polynomial reduction requires $O(M^2)$ bit-vector operations ($M \approx 10^4$), injecting a $10^5\times$ computational penalty per deduction step.

\subsection{Exp 5.1: AVX2 BCP Loop Unrolling \& Instruction Cache Bloat}
\paragraph{1. Architecture Implemented.}
Aggressive 256-bit SIMD loop unrolling for Boolean Constraint Propagation (\texttt{flat_cdcl_avx2/}). Attempted to accelerate the two-watched-literal sweep by unrolling literal evaluations 8-wide using AVX2 \texttt{\_mm256\_cmpeq\_epi32} instructions and manual branch peeling.

\paragraph{2. Exact Empirical Results.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Microbenchmark Speed:} On synthetic 3-SAT loops, SIMD throughput increased by $1.8\times$.
    \item \textbf{Industrial CNF Performance:} On full SAT Competition benchmarks, runtime was **$7.4\times$ slower** than baseline.
    \item \textbf{Tail Latency Spikes:} 99th percentile decision latency spiked from $1.8\,\mu\text{s}$ to $14.2\,\mu\text{s}$.
    \item \textbf{Instruction Cache Profiling:} Hardware counters (\texttt{perf stat}) revealed L1 Instruction Cache (L1i) miss rate surged from $0.4\%$ to **$28.6\%$**.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Mathematical Proof of Failure: Instruction Footprint Exceeding L1i Cache.}
Let $C_{\text{L1i}}$ be the CPU L1 instruction cache size ($32\text{ KB}$ on modern x86 cores). Unrolling the 2-watched-literal loop with 8-way AVX2 vectorization, clause header prefetching, and multi-condition branch exits expanded the binary footprint of the BCP routine from $4.2\text{ KB}$ to **$78.4\text{ KB}$**. Because $78.4\text{ KB} > C_{\text{L1i}} = 32\text{ KB}$, the inner loop cannot remain resident in L1i cache. Every call to BCP incurs instruction cache misses, causing CPU instruction-fetch pipeline stalls ($\sim 12\text{ cycles}$) that destroy all vectorization gains.

\subsection{Exp 5.2: WalkSAT Stochastic Local Search Restarts}
\paragraph{1. Architecture Implemented.}
Hybrid WalkSAT local search injection every 5,000 conflicts (\texttt{walksat_hybrid.c}).

\paragraph{2. Exact Empirical Results.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{UNSAT Slowdown:} Incurred a **$212\times$ slowdown** on UNSAT benchmarks; average conflict clause LBD degraded from $3.2$ to $8.6$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Mathematical Proof of Failure: Incomplete Local Search Barrier.}
Stochastic local search cannot produce refutation proofs ($\square$): $\forall t < \infty, \text{Pr}(\text{SLS proves UNSAT}) = 0$. On UNSAT formulas, cycles allocated to WalkSAT are pure waste that invalidate trail cache locality.

\subsection{Exp 5.3: Parallel Concurrent Multi-Threaded BCP Lock Contention}
\paragraph{1. Architecture Implemented.}
Multi-threaded CDCL with shared watched lists protected by spinlocks (\texttt{42_KISSAT_COOPERATIVE_DUAL_CORE}).

\paragraph{2. Exact Empirical Results.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Utilization:} **$35.4\%$ CPU idle time** waiting on spinlocks; speedup was only **$1.03\times$** on 2 full cores.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Mathematical Proof of Failure: MESI Invalidation Storms.}
Modifying watched literal pointers in shared cache lines at $10^7$ times/sec saturates the interconnect bus with MESI invalidation broadcasts, causing L2/L3 miss latency stalls that negate parallelism.
